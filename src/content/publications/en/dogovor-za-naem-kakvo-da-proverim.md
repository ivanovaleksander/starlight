The lease agreement is one of the most common contracts in everyday life, which is precisely why it is often signed without a careful read. The Bulgarian Obligations and Contracts Act sets out the basic rights and obligations of the parties, but a large part of the terms is determined by the agreement itself. Here is what you should check before you commit.

## 1. Who is signing the agreement

Make sure that the person letting the property actually has the right to do so: the owner, a co-owner with the necessary consent of the others, or a representative with an express power of attorney. For a property in co-ownership, the signature of only one co-owner may lead to disputes about the validity and effect of the agreement. Ask for the title document and an identity document and check the details.

## 2. Precise description of the property and its condition

The agreement should identify the property unambiguously: address, floor, area, ancillary rooms, furniture and appliances. It is good practice to sign a **handover protocol** on delivery, describing the condition and the meter readings. The protocol is the main evidence in any dispute about damage or unpaid utilities when the property is vacated.

## 3. Rent, due date and deposit

Check:

- the amount of the rent and whether utilities are included;
- the date by which each monthly instalment is due and the method of payment (bank transfer is recommended for evidential purposes);
- the conditions under which the rent may be increased;
- the amount of the deposit, when and how it is returned and what it may be withheld for.

The deposit secures the tenant's obligations. The agreement should clearly state that it is returned upon proper performance and within what period after the property is vacated.

## 4. Term and termination

The agreement may be for a fixed or an indefinite term. It should be clear:

- whether either party may terminate early and with what notice;
- what the consequences of early departure are (for example, forfeiture of the deposit or rent due for the notice period);
- what happens after the term expires if the tenant continues to use the property.

The law limits the maximum duration of a lease in certain cases, and for longer terms registering the agreement provides protection if the property changes hands.

## 5. Costs, repairs and improvements

Distinguish between **minor repairs** connected with ordinary use, which are usually borne by the tenant, and **major repairs**, which are the landlord's responsibility. If the tenant plans improvements (for example, new flooring), agree in advance who pays for them and whether their value is deducted from the rent.

## 6. Subletting and use

Check whether subletting or use by other persons is permitted, and whether there are restrictions on pets, business activity or registering the address.

## 7. Practical tips

- Do not sign an agreement you have not read in full, including the annexes.
- Keep all payments and correspondence in writing.
- If in doubt about a specific clause, seek legal advice before signing rather than after a dispute arises.
