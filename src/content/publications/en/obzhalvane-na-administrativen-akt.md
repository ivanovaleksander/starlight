Administrative acts affect the daily lives of individuals and companies: refusals to issue permits, penalty decrees, acts of the municipal administration, decisions on applications. The Bulgarian Administrative Procedure Code (APC) provides two main routes of protection, and the most common reason for losing a dispute is a **missed deadline**.

## 1. Identify the act and the date you were notified

The first step is to establish:

- which authority issued the act and on what grounds;
- whether the act is individual, general or normative, as the appeal procedure differs;
- **the date on which the act was notified to you** – the deadlines run from that date.

Notification may be in person, by post, by email or in another manner provided by law. Keep the envelope, the return receipt or the electronic message.

## 2. Administrative appeal

An individual administrative act may be challenged before the **superior administrative authority**. The appeal is filed through the authority that issued the act within 14 days of notification, unless a special law provides otherwise. This route is faster and cheaper, but it is not a mandatory precondition for judicial protection unless the law expressly requires it.

## 3. Judicial appeal

The act may also be challenged before the **competent administrative court**. The deadline is again 14 days from notification, and in the case of a tacit refusal, one month from the expiry of the period within which the authority should have decided. The appeal is filed through the authority that issued the act, which forwards it to the court together with the file.

## 4. What the appeal must contain

- details of the appellant and of the contested act;
- the grounds of unlawfulness: lack of competence, breach of form, material breach of procedure, conflict with substantive law, inconsistency with the purpose of the law;
- the request to the court – annulment, amendment or a declaration of nullity;
- the evidence and annexes.

A clear and structured presentation of the act's defects significantly improves the chances of success.

## 5. Is enforcement suspended

As a rule, filing an appeal **suspends enforcement** of the act, unless the law or the authority has allowed preliminary enforcement. In that case the court may be asked to suspend preliminary enforcement if it would cause significant or hard-to-repair harm.

## 6. Course of the court proceedings

The court reviews the lawfulness of the act, gathers evidence, hears the parties and delivers a judgment. The first-instance judgment is usually subject to cassation appeal before the Supreme Administrative Court within 14 days of notification. If the challenge succeeds, the costs incurred may be awarded against the administrative authority.

## 7. Practical tips

- Do not delay: the deadlines are short and are not easily restored.
- Collect all documents on the file, including your applications and the replies received.
- Seek advice before filing – correcting omissions after the deadline is often impossible.
